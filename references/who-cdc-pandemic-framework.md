# WHO/CDC Pandemic Preparedness Planning Framework

## Overview

This document extracts operational principles from WHO and CDC pandemic preparedness guidance, translating them into concrete procedures and checklists for pandemic-specific emergency planning. It implements the frameworks established by:

- WHO (2019). Pandemic Influenza Risk Management: A WHO Guide
- CDC (2021). Interim Infection Prevention and Control Recommendations
- WHO (2005). International Health Regulations (2005)

## Core Framework Principles

### 1. Prevention and Preparedness
- **Vaccination**: Stay current on recommended vaccinations
- **Hygiene infrastructure**: Maintain supplies for proper hygiene
- **Monitoring systems**: Establish methods for tracking disease spread
- **Planning assumptions**: Plan for extended disruptions (8-12 weeks minimum)

### 2. Detection and Response
- **Early recognition**: Identify symptoms and exposure risks early
- **Isolation procedures**: Implement immediate isolation for symptomatic individuals
- **Contact identification**: Track and notify potential exposures
- **Testing protocols**: Establish when and how to seek testing

### 3. Mitigation Strategies
- **Physical distancing**: Maintain distance from non-household members
- **Face coverings**: Use appropriate masks in public settings
- **Ventilation**: Improve indoor air quality
- **Hand hygiene**: Regular and proper hand washing

### 4. Recovery and Adaptation
- **Phased return**: Gradual return to normal activities
- **Continued precautions**: Maintain protective measures during recovery
- **Mental health**: Address psychological impacts
- **Lessons learned**: Update plans based on experience

## Pandemic Supply Kit

### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
- **Masks**: N95 or KN95 respirators (minimum 5 per person)
- **Surgical masks**: For everyday use (minimum 20 per person)
- **Gloves**: Disposable gloves for specific situations
- **Hand sanitizer**: 60%+ alcohol content (minimum 1 liter per household)
- **Soap**: Antibacterial soap for hand washing

### Hygiene Supplies
- **Disinfectant**: EPA-approved disinfectants for surfaces
- **Cleaning supplies**: Paper towels, spray bottles, sponges
- **Laundry**: Laundry detergent for frequent washing
- **Trash bags**: Heavy-duty bags for contaminated waste
- **Tissues**: For respiratory hygiene

### Medical Supplies
- **Thermometer**: Digital thermometer for fever monitoring
- **Pulse oximeter**: For oxygen saturation monitoring
- **Medications**:
  - Pain/fever reducers (acetaminophen, ibuprofen)
  - Cold/flu remedies
  - Prescription medications (30-day supply minimum)
- **First aid**: Basic first aid supplies

### Communication and Monitoring
- **Device charging**: Portable power banks for phones
- **Information sources**: Reliable health authority contacts
- **Tracking materials**: Notebook for symptom tracking
- **Backup communication**: Alternative communication methods

### Isolation Supplies
- **Designated room supplies**: Separate eating and drinking utensils
- **Bedding**: Separate linens for sick room
- **Waste disposal**: Dedicated waste container for sick room
- **Cleaning**: Separate cleaning supplies for sick room

### Infection Prevention Procedures

#### Hand Hygiene Protocol
1. **When to wash hands**
   - Before and after eating
   - After using the bathroom
   - After coughing, sneezing, or blowing nose
   - After being in public spaces
   - After caring for sick individuals
   - After touching pets or animals

2. **Proper hand washing technique**
   - Wet hands with clean water
   - Apply soap and lather thoroughly (20 seconds minimum)
   - Scrub all surfaces: backs of hands, between fingers, under nails
   - Rinse well with clean water
   - Dry with clean towel or air dry
   - Apply hand sanitizer if soap unavailable (60%+ alcohol)

3. **Hand sanitizer use**
   - Apply enough to cover all hand surfaces
   - Rub hands together until dry (20 seconds)
   - Do not wipe or rinse off before drying
   - Not effective on visibly dirty hands

#### Respiratory Hygiene
1. **Coughing and sneezing etiquette**
   - Cover mouth and nose with elbow or tissue
   - Use tissue and dispose immediately in lined trash can
   - Wash hands immediately after
   - Wear mask if coughing/sneezing cannot be controlled

2. **Mask wearing guidelines**
   - Use N95/KN95 for high-risk situations
   - Ensure proper fit (nose wire, chin coverage)
   - Handle only by ear loops
   - Replace when damaged, wet, or soiled
   - Do not touch mask while wearing
   - Wash hands before putting on and after removing

3. **Face mask care**
   - Store in clean, breathable container when not in use
   - Wash cloth masks daily
   - Do not reuse disposable masks
   - Replace disposable masks after each use

### Environmental Cleaning

#### Daily Cleaning Routine
1. **High-touch surfaces** (clean daily)
   - Doorknobs and handles
   - Light switches
   - Countertops and tables
   - Remote controls and electronics
   - Faucets and toilet handles
   - Keys and phones

2. **Cleaning procedure**
   - Wash hands before starting
   - Clean surface with soap and water
   - Apply disinfectant according to label directions
   - Allow proper contact time (usually 1-10 minutes)
   - Wipe clean if required
   - Wash hands after completing

3. **Laundry procedures**
   - Handle sick person's laundry with gloves if possible
   - Do not shake laundry (disperse virus)
   - Wash using warmest appropriate water setting
   - Dry thoroughly
   - Clean and disinfect laundry hampers

### Isolation Procedures

#### Designated Sick Room Setup
1. **Room selection**
   - Separate room with door
   - Ideally with dedicated bathroom
   - Good ventilation (open window if possible)
   - Away from common household areas

2. **Room supplies**
   - Dedicated eating and drinking utensils
   - Separate bedding and towels
   - Waste container with lid
   - Cleaning supplies for room only
   - Communication method (phone, tablet)

3. **Bathroom procedures (if shared)**
   - Sick person uses last, after others
   - Clean after each use by sick person
   - Ventilate well (open window, exhaust fan)
   - Remove unnecessary items (toothbrush holder, etc.)
   - Dedicate towels for sick person

4. **Food delivery**
   - Leave food at door
   - Use disposable or dedicated dishes
   - No person-to-person contact
   - Wash dishes after collection using gloves

#### Monitoring Protocol
1. **Daily symptom tracking** (for sick and potentially exposed)
   - Temperature (check twice daily)
   - Respiratory symptoms
   - Other symptoms (body aches, headache, etc.)
   - Oxygen saturation (if available)

2. **Red flag symptoms** (seek immediate care)
   - Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
   - Persistent pain or pressure in chest
   - New confusion or inability to wake/stay awake
   - Pale, gray, or blue-colored skin, lips, nail beds
   - Oxygen saturation below 94%

3. **Progression tracking**
   - Record temperature and symptoms daily
   - Note changes in severity
   - Track symptom onset dates
   - Monitor for new symptoms

### Communication During Pandemic

#### Information Sources
1. **Official health authorities**
   - Local health department website/phone
   - CDC (cdc.gov)
   - WHO (who.int)
   - State health department

2. **Avoid misinformation**
   - Verify information from official sources
   - Be cautious of social media claims
   - Check dates of information (can be outdated)
   - Look for scientific consensus

#### Household Communication
1. **Regular updates**
   - Daily check-ins on symptoms and status
   - Share reliable information
   - Discuss concerns openly
   - Update plans based on new information

2. **External communication**
   - Designate one person for external communication
   - Use phone, text, or video calls instead of visits
   - Inform visitors of household status
   - Coordinate assistance if needed

### Community Considerations

#### Mutual Aid
1. **Safe assistance**
   - Coordinate through phone or online
   - Leave supplies at door (no contact)
   - Wear mask and gloves if preparing care packages
   - Wash hands before and after

2. **Requesting help**
   - Identify specific needs
   - Communicate clearly and safely
   - Express gratitude
   - Reciprocate when able

#### Essential Activities
1. **Shopping**
   - Shop during less busy hours
   - Send only one household member if possible
   - Wear mask and practice hand hygiene
   - Use delivery services when available
   - Wipe down groceries if concerned

2. **Medical care**
   - Call ahead before visiting healthcare facilities
   - Use telemedicine options when appropriate
   - Follow healthcare facility instructions
   - Bring list of medications and conditions

## Special Considerations

### High-Risk Individuals
- **Extra precautions**: Consider stricter isolation measures
- **Early consultation**: Contact healthcare provider early if symptomatic
- **Medication supply**: Ensure adequate supply of all medications
- **Monitoring**: More frequent symptom monitoring

### Children and Schools
- **Age-appropriate communication**: Explain situations without causing fear
- **Routine maintenance**: Maintain consistent schedules
- **Social connection**: Facilitate safe social interaction
- **Screen time**: Balance educational and recreational use

### Mental Health
- **Routine**: Maintain daily structure and schedule
- **Connection**: Stay connected with support network remotely
- **Activity**: Regular physical activity at home
- **Information**: Limit news consumption to reliable sources
- **Professional help**: Seek help if experiencing severe anxiety or depression

### Economic Considerations
- **Budget planning**: Plan for reduced income or increased expenses
- **Financial assistance**: Identify available assistance programs
- **Supply prioritization**: Focus on essential supplies first
- **Community resources**: Connect with local mutual aid networks
