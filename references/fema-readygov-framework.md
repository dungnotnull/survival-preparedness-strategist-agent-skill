# FEMA/Ready.gov Household Emergency Preparedness Framework

## Overview

The FEMA Ready.gov framework provides the foundational methodology for household emergency preparedness in the United States. This reference document extracts the operational principles from the framework and translates them into concrete checklists and procedures for the Survival Preparedness Strategist skill.

## Core Principles

### 1. Be Informed
- **Monitor local emergency alerts**: Sign up for local alert systems and monitor emergency broadcasts
- **Know specific risks for your area**: Identify hazards most likely to affect your community
- **Understand warning systems**: Familiarize yourself with local warning signals (sirens, texts, etc.)
- **Identify evacuation routes**: Plan multiple evacuation routes from your home and workplace

### 2. Make a Plan
- **Family communication plan**: Establish how family members will contact each other if separated
- **Meeting locations**: Designate primary and secondary meeting places
- **Emergency contacts**: Maintain list of emergency contacts and out-of-area contacts
- **Special considerations**: Account for elderly, disabled, children, pets, and those with special needs

### 3. Build a Kit
- **Emergency supply kit**: Maintain supplies for at least 72 hours
- **Go-bag**: Portable kit for immediate evacuation
- **Vehicle kit**: Supplies for each vehicle
- **Workplace kit**: Supplies for workplace or school

## 72-Hour Emergency Supply Kit

### Water and Food
- **Water**: 1 gallon per person per day (for drinking and sanitation)
- **Food**: 3-day supply of non-perishable food
  - Ready-to-eat canned meats, fruits, vegetables
  - Protein or fruit bars
  - Dry cereal or granola
  - Peanut butter and crackers
  - Dried fruit and nuts

### Medical and First Aid
- **First aid kit**: Bandages, antiseptic, pain relievers, prescription medications
- **Medical supplies**: Thermometer, tweezers, scissors, latex gloves
- **Hygiene items**: Hand sanitizer, moist towelettes, garbage bags
- **Prescription medications**: At least 7-day supply

### Tools and Supplies
- **Flashlight**: With extra batteries
- **Radio**: Battery-powered or hand-crank NOAA weather radio
- **Cell phone charger**: Portable power bank
- **Multi-tool**: Basic hand tools and pliers
- **Can opener**: Manual can opener for canned food

### Important Documents
- **Identification**: Copies of IDs, insurance policies, medical records
- **Financial**: Cash, credit cards, bank account information
- **Contact information**: Emergency contacts, family phone numbers
- **Storage**: Waterproof container or bag

### Clothing and Bedding
- **Clothing**: One change of clothing per person
- **Footwear**: Sturdy walking shoes
- **Bedding**: Blankets or sleeping bags
- **Weather gear**: Rain gear, warm clothing for cold climates

### Special Items
- **Infant needs**: Diapers, formula, bottles
- **Pet supplies**: Food, water, leash, carrier, medications
- **Disability needs**: Extra medical equipment, batteries, supplies
- **Eyewear**: Glasses or contact lenses with solution

## Extended Preparedness (2+ Weeks)

### Water Storage
- **Quantity**: 1 gallon per person per day minimum
- **Storage**: Food-grade containers, away from light and heat
- **Rotation**: Replace stored water every 6 months
- **Treatment**: Water purification tablets or bleach as backup

### Food Storage
- **Calories**: 2,000+ calories per person per day
- **Variety**: Include different food groups and textures
- **Storage**: Cool, dry, dark place; proper food-grade containers
- **Rotation**: First-in, first-out system; track expiration dates

### Medical Preparedness
- **Prescription medications**: 30-day supply minimum
- **Over-the-counter medications**: Pain relievers, cold/flu, stomach remedies
- **Medical equipment**: Backup power for medical devices
- **Training**: Basic first aid and CPR certification

## Special Considerations

### Elderly or Disabled
- **Medical dependencies**: Identify medical equipment and backup power needs
- **Mobility limitations**: Plan evacuation assistance needed
- **Medication storage**: Ensure adequate medication supply
- **Support network**: Identify helpers and check-in procedures

### Children
- **Age-appropriate information**: Explain preparedness without causing fear
- **School plans**: Understand school emergency procedures
- **Identification**: Include recent photo and identifying information
- **Comfort items**: Include favorite toy or blanket in emergency kit

### Pets
- **Identification**: Tags, microchips, recent photos
- **Carrier/crate**: Proper sized carrier for each pet
- **Supplies**: Food, water, medications, litter, leash
- **Planning**: Pet-friendly shelters, veterinarians, boarding options

## Evacuation Planning

### Before Evacuation
1. **Monitor alerts**: Stay informed about developing situations
2. **Prepare vehicle**: Keep gas tank at least half full
3. **Prepare go-bag**: Ensure portable emergency kit is ready
4. **Secure home**: Lock doors/windows, unplug appliances, turn off utilities if instructed
5. **Communicate**: Notify family members of plan and status

### During Evacuation
1. **Follow instructions**: Follow official evacuation routes and orders
2. **Avoid shortcuts**: Stay on recommended roads
3. **Monitor updates**: Listen to emergency broadcasts
4. **Check on neighbors**: Assist those who may need help (safely)
5. **Report status**: Check in with out-of-area contact

### After Evacuation
1. **Wait for all-clear**: Only return when authorities say it's safe
2. **Inspect home**: Check for damage before entering
3. **Document damage**: Take photos for insurance
4. **Contact insurers**: Begin claims process
5. **Check utilities**: Have utilities inspected before use
