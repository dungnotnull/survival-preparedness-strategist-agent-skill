# The Rule of Threes: Survival Prioritization Framework

## Overview

The "Rule of Threes" is a fundamental survival prioritization framework that establishes the critical hierarchy of human needs in emergency situations. This document translates the wilderness/urban survival framework into concrete operational procedures for emergency preparedness planning.

## The Rule of Threes

The rule states that a human can survive:

- **3 minutes without oxygen**
- **3 hours without shelter (in extreme conditions)**
- **3 days without water**
- **3 weeks without food**

This hierarchy establishes the priority order for addressing survival needs: Air → Shelter → Water → Food.

## Implementation in Preparedness Planning

### Priority 1: Air and Immediate Safety

#### Air Quality Concerns
1. **Smoke and fire**
   - Stay low to the ground (smoke rises)
   - Use cloth or mask to filter air
   - Evacuate immediately from smoke-filled areas
   - Check doors for heat before opening

2. **Chemical/biological hazards**
   - Seal room if advised (turn off HVAC, tape windows)
   - Wait for official all-clear before leaving
   - Follow official evacuation orders immediately
   - Do not attempt to rescue others if it puts you at risk

3. **Carbon monoxide**
   - Install CO detectors in home
   - Never use generators/heat sources indoors
   - Recognize symptoms: headache, dizziness, nausea
   - Evacuate immediately if CO suspected

4. **Dust/debris**
   - Cover nose and mouth with cloth
   - Wear N95 mask if available
   - Protect eyes with goggles or glasses
   - Move away from dust source

### Priority 2: Shelter and Thermoregulation

#### Core Temperature Management
1. **Hypothermia prevention** (body temp drops below 95°F/35°C)
   - **Recognize symptoms**:
     - Shivering (early stage)
     - Confusion, slurred speech (moderate)
     - Drowsiness, weak pulse (severe)

   - **Treatment**:
     - Move to warm, dry location
     - Remove wet clothing
     - Warm with blankets (skin-to-skin contact effective)
     - Provide warm, non-alcoholic beverages
     - **Do not** rub affected areas (causes tissue damage)

2. **Heat illness prevention**
   - **Heat exhaustion symptoms**:
     - Heavy sweating, weakness
     - Cold, pale, clammy skin
     - Fast, weak pulse
     - Nausea, vomiting

   - **Heat stroke symptoms**:
     - Body temp above 103°F
     - Hot, red, dry or damp skin
     - Fast, strong pulse
     - Possible unconsciousness

   - **Treatment**:
     - Move to cooler location
     - Apply cool, wet cloths
     - Drink water if conscious
     - **Heat stroke**: Seek immediate medical attention

3. **Shelter priorities**
   - **Protection from elements**: Wind, rain, sun, cold
   - **Insulation from ground**: Ground draws heat 25x faster than air
   - **Ventilation**: Essential for fire safety and air quality
   - **Signaling**: Ability to signal for help

4. **Shelter construction principles**
   - **Start immediately**: Don't wait until dark or cold
   - **Size matters**: Small shelter retains heat better
   - **Elevate bedding**: At least 6 inches off ground
   - **Insulate**: Use dry leaves, pine needles, clothing
   - **Windbreak**: Block prevailing winds
   - **Water runoff**: Ensure water won't flow into shelter

### Priority 3: Water (3 Days Without)

#### Water Procurement
1. **Sources by priority**:
   - Stored water (safest)
   - Municipal water (check advisories)
   - Wells (test regularly)
   - Rainwater (must be treated)
   - Surface water (must be treated - highest risk)

2. **Collection methods**:
   - **Rainwater**: Clean surfaces, collection containers
   - **Dew**: Tie cloth around legs, walk through grass in morning
   - **Snow/ice**: Melt before consuming (don't eat snow directly)
   - **Transpiration**: Bag vegetation, collect condensation
   - **Groundwater**: Solar stills, digging in damp areas

#### Water Purification Methods

**By effectiveness and reliability:**

1. **Boiling** (most reliable)
   - Bring to rolling boil
   - Boil for 1 minute at sea level, 3 minutes at high elevation
   - Let cool before storing
   - **Pros**: Kills all pathogens, no chemicals needed
   - **Cons**: Requires fuel, time

2. **Chemical treatment** (reliable backup)
   - **Chlorine bleach**: 8 drops per gallon (6% sodium hypochlorite)
   - **Iodine tablets**: Follow package directions
   - **Wait time**: 30 minutes for chlorine, up to 4 hours for cold water with iodine
   - **Pros**: Lightweight, effective against most pathogens
   - **Cons**: Doesn't kill Cryptosporidium (chlorine), taste

3. **Filtration** (when available)
   - **Filter specifications**: Must remove 0.1 micron particles
   - **Types**:
     - Pump filters (ceramic, hollow fiber)
     - Gravity filters (ceramic, carbon)
     - Straw filters (personal use)
   - **Pros**: No taste, immediate use
   - **Cons**: Can clog, may not remove viruses

4. **UV treatment** (emergency only)
   - Solar disinfection (SODIS): Clear bottle in sun for 6 hours
   - UV pens: Battery powered
   - **Pros**: No chemicals
   - **Cons**: Requires clear water, time

#### Water Storage
1. **Container requirements**:
   - Food-grade material only
   - Clean, sanitized containers
   - Tight-fitting caps
   - Dark or opaque (light promotes algae)

2. **Storage guidelines**:
   - **Location**: Cool, dark place away from chemicals
   - **Rotation**: Every 6 months
   - **Quantity**: 1 gallon per person per day minimum
   - **Labeling**: Date containers

3. **Preparation**:
   - Clean containers with soap, rinse
   - Sanitize with bleach solution (1 teaspoon per quart)
   - Rinse thoroughly
   - Fill with treated water
   - Seal tightly

#### Water Conservation
1. **Daily consumption priorities**:
   - Drinking: 0.5-1 gallon per day
   - Sanitation: 1-2 gallons per day
   - Cooking: Minimal additional water needed

2. **Conservation techniques**:
   - Don't flush toilets with drinking water (use other water)
   - Use sanitizing wipes instead of water for cleaning
   - No-flush toilet methods (bucket + liner)
   - Minimal washing (spot clean, full body less frequently)
   - Reuse cooking water

3. **Emergency finding** (when stored water exhausted):
   - Water heater: Drain from bottom valve
   - Toilet tank (not bowl): Only if bowl water is not chemically treated
   - Pipes: Open lowest faucet, collect from highest
   - Canned foods: Liquid in canned goods

### Priority 4: Food (3 Weeks Without)

#### Caloric Requirements
1. **Basal metabolic rate**:
   - Adult male: 1,500-2,000 calories/day
   - Adult female: 1,200-1,500 calories/day
   - Children: 1,000-1,800 calories/day (varies by age)
   - Increases with activity, stress, cold

2. **Emergency minimum**:
   - Survival minimum: 1,000-1,200 calories/day
   - Not sustainable long-term but prevents starvation
   - Should be balanced for nutrition if possible

#### Food Storage Principles
1. **Requirements for emergency food**:
   - Non-perishable (no refrigeration)
   - Ready to eat or minimal preparation
   - High calorie-to-weight ratio
   - Familiar foods (reduces stress)
   - Variety (morale and nutrition)

2. **Food categories**:
   - **Grains/starches** (40% of calories):
     - Rice, pasta, crackers
     - Flour, cornmeal
     - Oats, cereals

   - **Proteins** (20% of calories):
     - Canned meats, fish
     - Beans, legumes
     - Nuts, seeds
     - Protein powder

   - **Fruits/vegetables** (25% of calories):
     - Canned fruits/vegetables
     - Dried fruits
     - Fruit/vegetable juices
     - Freeze-dried

   - **Fats** (15% of calories):
     - Oil (olive, coconut)
     - Butter (shelf-stable)
     - Peanut butter
     - Nuts

3. **Storage guidelines**:
   - **Location**: Cool, dry, dark place
   - **Temperature**: Below 70°F, ideally below 60°F
   - **Containers**: Food-grade, pest-proof
   - **Organization**: First-in, first-out (FIFO)
   - **Rotation**: Before expiration dates

4. **Preparation considerations**:
   - **Cooking methods**: Requires fuel, equipment
   - **Water needs**: Dehydrated foods require water
   - **Tools needed**: Can opener, cookware
   - **Backup plans**: Foods that can be eaten cold

#### Emergency Food Procurement
1. **Nature sources** (emergency only):
   - **Wild edibles**: Only if positively identified
   - **Fishing**: Requires equipment and skill
   - **Hunting/trapping**: Requires significant skill
   - **Foraging**: Dangerous without expert knowledge

2. **Urban sources** (after disaster):
   - Damaged stores (as last resort)
   - Vending machines
   - Food previously discarded
   - **Caution**: Food safety uncertain

3. **Rationing strategies**:
   - Reduce caloric intake gradually
   - One meal per day
   - Avoid complete fasting (metabolism shutdown)
   - Preserve water balance

## Integration with Preparedness Planning

### Using Rule of Threes in Planning

1. **Tier 1: Air/Safety (Immediate)**
   - [ ] Install smoke detectors and CO detectors
   - [ ] Establish evacuation routes
   - [ ] Identify safe rooms for shelter-in-place
   - [ ] Practice evacuation procedures

2. **Tier 2: Shelter (Hours)**
   - [ ] Maintain appropriate clothing for season
   - [ ] Have emergency blankets
   - [ ] Plan for alternative shelter locations
   - [ ] Understand thermoregulation principles

3. **Tier 3: Water (Days)**
   - [ ] Store minimum 3-day water supply
   - [ ] Have water treatment options
   - [ ] Practice water conservation
   - [ ] Know multiple water sources

4. **Tier 4: Food (Weeks)**
   - [ ] Store minimum 3-day food supply
   - [ ] Have 2-week supply for extended emergencies
   - [ ] Understand caloric needs
   - [ ] Plan food preparation methods

### Priority Decision Tree

**When facing an emergency situation:**

1. **Is the air safe to breathe?**
   - No → Address immediately, evacuate or shelter
   - Yes → Move to next priority

2. **Is shelter from elements needed?**
   - Yes (extreme hot/cold, weather) → Establish shelter immediately
   - No → Move to next priority

3. **Is water available?**
   - No (less than 3-day supply) → Secure and purify water
   - Yes → Move to next priority

4. **Is food available?**
   - No (less than 3-week supply) → Plan food procurement
   - Yes → Maintain supplies

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Prioritizing food over water**
   - Error: Focusing on food storage while neglecting water
   - Correction: Water always takes priority over food

2. **Neglecting shelter needs**
   - Error: Assuming shelter is always available
   - Correction: Plan for alternative shelter in all conditions

3. **Air quality complacency**
   - Error: Not considering air hazards (smoke, gas, CO)
   - Correction: Include air safety in all plans

4. **Improper prioritization**
   - Error: Addressing lower priorities before critical ones
   - Correction: Always follow Rule of Threes hierarchy

## Special Considerations

### Climate Adjustments
1. **Cold climates**
   - Shelter becomes more urgent than in temperate zones
   - Caloric needs increase significantly
   - Water still critical (dehydration risk in cold)

2. **Hot climates**
   - Water needs increase dramatically
   - Heat illness prevention critical
   - Shelter for shade becomes urgent

3. **Urban vs. rural**
   - Urban: Different shelter challenges, water procurement
   - Rural: More isolation, different resource access

### Health Conditions
1. **Medical dependencies**
   - Oxygen: Highest priority (air category)
   - Temperature-sensitive medications
   - Special dietary needs

2. **Vulnerable populations**
   - Children: Higher metabolic rate, less reserve
   - Elderly: More susceptible to temperature extremes
   - Disabled: May have specialized equipment needs

## Decision-Making Framework

**When resource-limited, use this hierarchy:**

1. **Secure air/safety** (non-negotiable)
2. **Establish shelter** (hours matters)
3. **Secure water** (days matter)
4. **Plan food** (weeks matter)

**Only move to next priority when current priority is addressed.**

**Examples:**
- Don't search for food if you only have 1 day of water left
- Don't purify water if you're freezing to death
- Don't store water if you have no shelter from freezing conditions

The Rule of Threes provides a clear, survival-validated framework for prioritizing emergency needs. Use it to guide all preparedness planning decisions.
